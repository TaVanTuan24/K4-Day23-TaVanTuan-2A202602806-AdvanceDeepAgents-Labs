# World Models: A Survey of Architectures, Evaluation and Open Problems

## TL;DR
- A world model is a learned predictive model of an environment that an agent can use to plan, control or simulate; the lineage runs from the 2018 recurrent World Models agent [1] through PlaNet's recurrent state-space model [2], Dreamer's actor-critic trained by latent imagination [3], and MuZero's decision-relevant value-equivalent model [4].
- The field has split into two live paradigms: reconstruction-based latent dynamics [2][3][5] versus reconstruction-free latent prediction, which wins concrete planning benchmarks — DINO-WM's frozen DINOv2 patch features reach 0.98 on Maze, 0.96 on Wall and 0.90 on PushT, while single-vector encoders score as low as 0.06 [6].
- Since 2024 the momentum has moved to action-conditioned generative world models: Genie's 11B-parameter latent-action model, trained on a filtered set of 30,000 hours of gameplay video drawn from a far larger pool [7], led to real-time 720p/24 FPS generation with roughly one minute of visual memory in Genie 3 [8], and open follow-ups such as Matrix-Game 2.0 streaming minute-level video at 25 FPS from about 1200 hours of engine data [9].
- The evaluations are overwhelmingly negative: Physics-IQ's best model scores 29.5 of a possible 100.0 against a real-video baseline [10], VideoPhy-2's best model reaches only 22% joint semantic-and-physical adherence on its hard subset [11], and WorldModelBench's top model finishes only 61% of tasks with 12% mass-conservation violations [12].
- Visual realism does not predict physical understanding: WorldModelBench finds only a low correlation of 0.28 between physics-adherence win rates and VBench's total score [12], and OOD generalization error "does not improve with increased data and model size" when scaling from 30K to 3 million examples and 22M to 310M parameters [13].

## Background
A world model is the internal, learned model of an environment that an agent uses instead of the environment itself. The canonical construction is Ha and Schmidhuber's 2018 agent, which trains a compressed spatial and temporal representation of an environment unsupervised and splits it into a large vision model (V), a dynamics model (M) whose LSTM is combined with a Mixture Density Network to predict a distribution over the next latent vector, and a deliberately tiny controller (C) trained by evolution strategies inside the "dream environment" generated by the world model [1]. That paper already names the two intellectual ancestors the field still cites: Schmidhuber's "Making the World Differentiable" (1990) and PILCO's Gaussian-process dynamics (2011) [1].

Why it matters now: world models are the mechanism behind sample-efficient control and behind the current claim that video generators are becoming general-purpose simulators of the physical world [14]. The efficiency case is quantified at the user end. PlaNet plans over latent states rather than images and reported performance comparable to the best model-free algorithms while using 200× fewer episodes [2], and Dreamer, which propagates analytic value gradients through trajectories imagined in the learned latent state space, "exceeds existing approaches in data-efficiency, computation time, and final performance" on 20 challenging visual control tasks [3]. Generality arrived with DreamerV3, described as a single configuration that outperforms specialized methods across over 150 diverse tasks and as the first algorithm to collect diamonds in Minecraft from scratch without human data or curricula [5]. MuZero took the orthogonal route of learning a model that predicts only the quantities most directly relevant to planning — reward, action-selection policy and value — achieving a new state of the art on 57 Atari games and matching AlphaZero on Go, chess and shogi without any knowledge of the game rules [4]. A comprehensive 2024 survey organises the whole literature by function rather than by architecture, splitting it into understanding world dynamics versus predicting future dynamics, with application sections on generative games, autonomous driving, robotics and social simulation [15]; more recent reviews do the same for single domains, positioning policy learning, planning and simulation as the embodied-robotics role of world models [16] and categorising driving world models by predicted scene modalities [17].

## Theme 1: Latent dynamics, imagination and decision-relevant models
The first generation of world models differs mainly in *what* the model is asked to predict, and that choice drives the reported results. PlaNet predicts latent states and reconstructs images and rewards from them, and its authors argue that both the deterministic and the stochastic transition components of the Recurrent State Space Model are crucial for high planning performance, with a multi-step "latent overshooting" objective encouraging consistency between one-step and multi-step predictions [2]. Dreamer keeps that reconstruction-based latent space but changes the use of it: behaviour is learned by propagating analytic gradients of learned state values through imagined trajectories, i.e. an actor-critic that never acts in the real environment during policy improvement [3]. DreamerV3 then attacks robustness rather than architecture, relying on normalization, balancing and transformations to hold a single hyperparameter configuration across over 150 tasks [5].

MuZero sits on the other side of a conceptual divide. Instead of reconstructing observations or latent states, its learned model is iterable and predicts reward, policy and value — quantities that matter for planning but need not describe the scene [4]. This "value-equivalent" model is what lets search operate without the game rules, but it also means the model cannot be inspected, reused as a simulator, or transferred as a representation. The JEPA line makes a third choice: I-JEPA predicts the *representations* of target blocks from a single context block, i.e. it is explicitly non-generative and works in representation space rather than pixel space, and its authors report that the two design decisions that matter are sampling target blocks at sufficiently large (semantic) scale and using a spatially distributed context block, with a ViT-Huge/14 trained on 16 A100 GPUs in under 72 hours [18].

Read together, the evidence supports a three-way contrast rather than a ranking: reconstruction-based latent models (PlaNet, Dreamer) buy an inspectable generative model and pay in pixels [2][3]; value-equivalent models (MuZero) buy search performance and give up simulation ability [4]; representation-predictive models (I-JEPA) buy semantic representations and defer the question of whether they can control anything [18].

## Theme 2: Interactive, action-conditioned generative world models (2024–2026)
The dominant recent shift is turning video generators into *interactive* models conditioned on actions. Genie is the anchor: an 11B-parameter unsupervised generative model that creates action-controllable virtual worlds from unlabelled videos using a spatiotemporal video tokenizer, an autoregressive dynamics model and a scalable latent action model, trained on a large dataset of publicly available Internet gaming videos with a final filtered set of 30,000 hours from hundreds of 2D platformer games, scaling the dynamics model from 40M to 2.7B parameters before the final 10.1B configuration trained for 125k steps on 256 TPUv5p [7]. Genie 3 is DeepMind's own 2025 successor: a text-prompted general-purpose world model that generates navigable worlds in real time at 24 frames per second, retaining consistency for a few minutes at 720p with visual memory extending as far back as one minute, and DeepMind describes that consistency as an emergent capability [8].

Open and applied variants converge on the same recipe with different engineering emphases. Matrix-Game 2.0 produces about 1200 hours of Unreal Engine and GTA5 video with diverse interaction annotations, adds an action injection module for frame-level mouse and keyboard inputs, and streams minute-level video at 25 FPS as an open-source release [9]. Vid2World attacks the conversion problem generically by causalizing a pre-trained video diffusion model — crafting its architecture and training objective for autoregressive generation — and adding a causal action guidance mechanism to improve action controllability [19]. PAN combines an autoregressive latent dynamics backbone based on a large language model with a video diffusion decoder, conditioning on history and natural-language actions, and claims strong performance on action-conditioned simulation, long-horizon forecasting and simulative reasoning relative to other video generators and world models [20]. Genie Envisioner packages the same idea for robotics as a platform: an instruction-conditioned video diffusion model (GE-Base), a lightweight flow-matching decoder that maps latents to executable action trajectories across embodiments (GE-Act), an action-conditioned neural simulator (GE-Sim) for closed-loop rollouts, and EWMBench for measuring visual fidelity, physical consistency and instruction-action alignment [21].

The most recent cohort on the Hugging Face daily feed shows the axis of competition moving from raw generation quality to *task success and interaction fidelity*. WorldGuide reformulates procedural generation as closed-loop task execution in visual world space — predict an atomic action, generate its clip, use the result to pick the next action — and reports 33.33% Task Success on its own ~59K-video, 245-task, 27-category WorldGuide-Bench versus 29.90% for MiniMax-H3 even though the baseline receives reference action plans, plus 47.69% versus 32.73% on VideoCraft-Bench [22]. DreamTrue, a multi-view cross-embodiment robot world model, reports reducing the human-assessed interaction defect rate from 48.12% to 6.25% on AgiBot and ranking first in the world model track of the AgiBot World Challenge 2026 [23]. ME-World pushes toward multi-agent egocentric generation by jointly denoising multiple ego streams in a shared token sequence with cross-view action consistency and shared-environment consistency, and introduces shared-world consistency metrics for environment, update and identity consistency [24]. SPW-Nav streams one minute of 2K 360-degree video in real time from a single panorama and honours language instructions as camera motion [25]. All of these interaction-focused entries appeared on the same recent daily-papers feed, indicating that current attention is aimed at interaction fidelity and measurable task success rather than at raw generation quality [22][23][24][25][26].

## Theme 3: Reconstruction versus reconstruction-free latent prediction
The sharpest architectural disagreement in current work is whether a world model should decode anything at all. The theory paper comparing the two paradigms gives the condition under which reconstruction loses: because joint-embedding methods impose a strictly weaker alignment condition than reconstruction, they are preferable when irrelevant features have a large magnitude, and it reports that under ImageNet-100 corruption from severity 1 to 5, MAE suffers a 25.1% average accuracy drop versus 10.5% for DINO and 12.4% for BYOL [27].

The empirical case comes from DINO-WM, which predicts future patch features of a frozen DINOv2 and never reconstructs the visual world: its patch-feature encoder reaches planning success of 0.98 on Maze, 0.96 on Wall and 0.90 on PushT, versus 0.42 (R3M), 0.20 (ResNet) and 0.44 (DINO CLS) on PushT — and the authors attribute the gap precisely to losing spatial details by reducing observations to a single global feature vector [6]. That is a strong result *and* a narrow one: the baselines are not world models at all, they are encoders plugged into the same planner.

Within the JEPA family the anti-collapse mechanism, not the prediction target, is where the current argument is. Sub-JEPA frames training as a bias-variance tradeoff — too few constraints cause representation collapse, too many suppress representation richness — and replaces LeWM's isotropic Gaussian prior with Gaussian constraints applied in multiple random subspaces, reporting that it "consistently outperforms LeWM with very clear margins" across four continuous-control environments and that orthogonal frozen projections beat random frozen projections, especially on PushT [28]. AC-MTM takes the opposite route and drops the Gaussian requirement altogether: it keeps LeWM's forward latent-prediction objective but adds a training-only Action-NCE inverse-dynamics head that is discarded after training, reaching 80.0±2.0% success on OGBench Visual Scene versus 58.0±2.0% for the SIGReg regularizer, with a 52% single 50-episode random-policy baseline; notably the authors report that it only *matches* SIGReg on four standard pixel-control tasks and that PushT exposes a real limitation of inverse-action signals [29]. ALeWM attacks the same problem from the capacity side, learning a sequence-conditioned distribution over latent prefix lengths so that predictive information concentrates in a compact prefix rather than being spread by generic anti-collapse objectives [30].

Two cautionary results bound the optimism. AD-E2E-JEPA systematically evaluates action-conditioned JEPA world models (LeWM, DINO-WM, JEPA-WM) for end-to-end autonomous driving in a goal-conditioned zero-shot planning setting that isolates model quality from policy learning, and its reported finding is that existing JEPA-based world models fall short [31]. And the "Critiques of World Models" paper argues that JEPA's latent reconstruction loss is upper-bounded by the generative data reconstruction loss plus a small encoder-decoder error, so minimizing the latent loss does not guarantee consistency with observed data, and that the evidence for practical usability "remains scarce as these models have mainly been demonstrated in toy environments with simple heuristics and action spaces" [32].

## Theme 4: How world models are evaluated, and what the numbers show
The evaluation literature is now the strongest evidence in the whole field, and it is unflattering to generative world models. Six independently built benchmarks agree in direction. VideoPhy curates material-interaction prompts and finds that the best-performing model, CogVideoX-5B, adheres to caption and physical laws in 39.6% of instances while other models are far behind at under 20% [33]. VideoPhy-2 moves to 200 curated actions and reports that even the best model achieves only 22% joint performance (high semantic *and* physical adherence) on the hard subset, with the best model Wan2.1-14B at 32.6% on the entire set, and that models particularly struggle with conservation laws like mass and momentum [11]. Physics-IQ builds 396 controlled videos spanning solid mechanics, fluid dynamics, optics, thermodynamics and magnetism and finds that against a real-video physical-variance baseline, the best model scores only 29.5 of a possible 100.0, with the conclusion that physical understanding is severely limited and unrelated to visual realism, and that visual realism does not imply physical understanding [10]. WorldModelBench covers 7 domains and 56 subdomains with 350 prompts and 67K human labels, and reports that the top-scoring model, KLING, finishes only 61% of specified tasks, with 12% of videos violating mass conservation and 11% synthesizing penetrating objects; its human scores run from 8.82 (KLING) down to 5.83 (OpenSora-I2V) against 9.97 for real videos [12]. PhyGround uses 250 curated prompts and a taxonomy of 13 physical laws, with 459 annotators providing 5,796 complete annotations and over 37.4K fine-grained labels at split-half model-ranking Spearman's rho above 0.90; its leaderboard tops out at 3.28 of 5 for both Wan2.2-27B-A14B and Veo-3.1, falling to 2.56 for the weakest entry [34]. World Models' Last Exam in Physics spans 40 controlled tasks and 1,280 generated videos across eight models, with the best model at 57.76 out of 100 [35].

Two methodological results matter more than any single score. First, proxies for quality are anti-correlated with the property that matters: WorldModelBench reports a high correlation of 0.69 between its frame-wise quality win rates and VBench but only a low correlation of 0.28 between its physics-adherence win rates and VBench's total score, and documents that Luma has higher frame-wise quality (0.81 versus 0.63) and temporal quality (0.76 versus 0.63) than mochi yet finishes the specified task less often (44% versus 53%) [12]. Second, automated judging is itself unreliable: GPT-4o's prediction error is 29.3% on instruction following, 35.0% on common sense and 36.0% on physics adherence, with a fine-tuned VILA-2B judge improving common-sense error only to 16.4% [12]; PhyGround's released judge PhyJudge-9B reports substantially lower aggregate relative bias than Gemini-3.1-Pro (3.3% versus 16.6%) [34].

Failure analysis has also become decomposed rather than aggregate. The event-boundary study evaluates prediction as a sequence of separable capabilities — consequence production, temporal anchoring and physical realization — over 62 controlled free-fall recordings and 124 clips: Runway and Veo carry a release through to a subsequent impact in over 93% of clips while Cosmos-3 and PhyWorld do so in roughly one third and Cosmos-Predict-2.5 never generates a release, yet even the good models place the consequence too late (0.25 s and 0.21 s versus a real release-to-impact duration of 0.20 s) and estimate gravity far below physics (PhyWorld 3.36±3.12 m/s², Runway 5.35±5.67, Veo 4.47±4.85 against the expected 9.81), leading to the conclusion that plausible timing does not imply physically consistent motion [36]. WorldReasonBench stress-tests the same capability over 436 curated cases in four reasoning dimensions and 22 subcategories, plus a WorldRewardBench preference set of about 6K expert-annotated pairs over 1.4K videos, and states the central finding plainly: videos can look convincing while failing dynamics, causality or information preservation [37]. One benchmark even reports contradictions *within* a benchmark: I2V variants are worse than their T2V counterparts on all three matched pairs (cogvideox 7.31 versus 6.75, opensoraplan 7.62 versus 6.62, opensora 6.11 versus 5.83) [12].

## Theme 5: Applications, embodiment and safety
Applied world models concentrate in robotics, driving and navigation, and each domain has produced its own survey: world models as predictive representations of environmental dynamics supporting policy learning, planning and simulation across embodied applications [16], and driving world models categorised by predicted scene modalities with their own datasets and metrics [17]. The most quantitatively interesting question in this cluster is transfer. The cross-embodiment work conjectures that environment dynamics are embodiment-invariant, represents embodiments as sets of 3D particles with actions as displacements, and reports that prediction error decreases as more embodiments are included while variance across subsets shrinks — with five training embodiments and no target data, performance often approaches or surpasses training directly on the target hand. The same study pinpoints the sim-to-real gap numerically: simulation-only training yields the highest prediction error, models trained solely on human data transfer to robot hands, and co-training simulation with real human data at a 1:1 ratio performs best, enabling a 6-DoF PSYONIC Ability Hand and a 12-DoF Robot Era XHand to perform deformable object manipulation [38].

Failure modes are now being named and, in one case, predicted. The hallucination study treats hallucination as primarily a data-coverage problem concentrated in low-coverage regions of the state-action space rather than an architecture problem, and anchors three failure modes to pipeline stages: perceptual hallucination in the encoder-decoder pair, action marginalization in the dynamics model, and scene divergence during multi-step rollouts — the last being the mechanism by which "autoregressive rollouts accumulate compounding error as the prediction horizon increases" [39]. Using a 350M-parameter Dreamer 4 model, it shows that hallucination-driven curiosity collection with just 50 real trajectories per task lifts closed-loop performance to 0.325 versus an expert/human oracle of 0.362 and a random-policy baseline of 0.118, with predictors tracking hallucination at rho ≈ 0.80 against rollout delta PSNR [39]. Genie 3's own authors concede the same structural weakness for their family: autoregressive environment generation is harder than generating a whole video because inaccuracies tend to accumulate over time [8].

Safety is the newest and thinnest application area. A security study of world models in agentic systems reports that attackers can induce mispredictions with up to 95% success rate, possibly resulting in unintended command execution, denial of service, wallet drainage and private-information extraction, and argues some risks are intrinsic to approximate world modeling: the model emits a single deterministic prediction rather than the space of possible interleavings for concurrent programs, and a model trained before GNU coreutils 9.10 has no basis for knowing that "-A" is a short option for "--apparent-size" [40]. The paper also notes that world-model security research is in its early stages and remains concentrated in robotics and continuous control [40].

Two adjacent approaches round out the landscape. The 3D/neural-rendering family builds explicit representations instead of predicting frames: HY-World 2.0 generates high-fidelity 3D Gaussian Splatting scenes from diverse inputs with dedicated modules for panorama generation, trajectory planning, world expansion and composition, drawing the most community attention among current 3D world-model entries [41]; OuroWorld turns any static 3D Gaussian Splatting scene into a 3D cinemagraph using a vision-language model to infer plausible dynamics and a Fourier-series deformation field that guarantees looping by construction, outperforming all baselines and winning 70.8%–99.0% of user-study comparisons on 39 scenes [42]. DeepMind's stated tradeoff between the two families is explicit: NeRFs and Gaussian Splatting allow consistent navigable 3D environments but depend on an explicit 3D representation, whereas Genie 3's frame-by-frame worlds are "far more dynamic and rich" at the cost of accumulating inaccuracies [8]. The LLM/VLM-as-simulator family is the least developed: VisPhyWorld probes physical reasoning in multimodal LLMs by requiring executable simulator code generation from visual observations, arguing this separates physical reasoning from rendering and makes world representations inspectable and falsifiable [43], while Memento 3 keeps a natural-language rulebook as world model for frozen LLM agents and reports clearing every level of all 25 public ARC-AGI-3 games at a mean Relative Human Action Efficiency of 100.0 using 44% of the human action count [26].

## Trends and open problems
What changed in the last two years is the centre of gravity. In 2023 the questions were about latent dynamics and generality [5][18]; from 2024 onward the field's flagship artefacts are action-conditioned generative world models measured on interactivity — Genie's 11B latent-action model over a large gaming-video corpus [7], Genie 3 at 24 FPS and 720p with one minute of visual memory [8], Matrix-Game 2.0 streaming at 25 FPS [9], and task-success-oriented entrants such as WorldGuide [22] and DreamTrue [23]. Reconstruction-free latent prediction also matured from a representation-learning idea [18] into a planning method with hard numbers [6][28][29], and evaluation caught up with both by becoming adversarial rather than celebratory [33][11][10][12].

The open problems are unusually well-documented. A 2026 state-of-the-field report names seven recurring problems and states that none is fully solved: the definition of a world model is still unclear, video generation is not world modeling by default, long-horizon reasoning is still weak, action conditioning is underdeveloped, sim-to-real transfer is hard, the data bottleneck persists, and safety is not optional — with video≠world-modeling and long-horizon reasoning both labelled critical [44].

Three of these are actively disputed rather than merely open. First, whether scaling video generation yields usable world models: OpenAI's position is that scaling video generation is a promising path towards general-purpose simulators of the physical world, with 3D consistency emerging without explicit inductive biases, while conceding that Sora does not accurately model many basic interactions such as glass shattering and develops incoherencies in long samples [14]; the controlled physical-law study answers directly that scaling alone is insufficient, since out-of-distribution generalization error does not improve when scaling data from 30K to 3 million examples and parameters from 22M to 310M, and reports that on unseen low-speed balls the model relies on memorisation and case-based imitation with a reference priority order of color >> size >> velocity >> shape [13]. Second, whether generative or latent models deserve the name at all: the "Critiques of World Models" paper argues that video generators only produce fixed trajectories and lack explicit notions of state, action and object-level representation and therefore fall outside the definition of world models for reasoning and planning, that contemporary systems' emphasis on video generation remains largely unexamined, and that a world model is fundamentally a sandbox for reasoning and thought-experiment; it also notes that interactive game world models have shallow temporal coherence because current generation horizons of 1–2 minutes fall far short of gameplay sessions spanning several hours [32]. Third, whether the JEPA family transfers: DINO-WM's large margins [6] sit awkwardly beside the finding that action-conditioned JEPA world models fall short in a zero-shot driving evaluation [31], the observation that latent-space losses do not guarantee data consistency [32], and the fact that the anti-collapse mechanism itself changes results by 20–24 points [29].

Compounding error is the most consistently reported unsolved mechanism, appearing in the DeepMind blog as a structural property of autoregressive generation [8], in the hallucination study as scene divergence during multi-step rollouts [39], in the state-of-the-field report as drift over time that makes state unstable for planning [44], and in the event-boundary study as timing that is plausible while motion is not [36]. Everything else — action conditioning at fine granularity [24], multi-agent consistency [24], cross-embodiment invariance [38], reliable automated judges [34][12], and security of world models inside agentic pipelines [40] — is early-stage evidence, not settled science.
